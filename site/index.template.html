What you are looking at

That lap is real data.

It came out of the lab's own simulator — the same code you run in Module 1 — streamed through the ATLAS Stream API into Kafka and read back out.

Every channel above arrived with its own name, its own units and its own sample rate, carried in the stream itself. Nothing on this page has those values hardcoded. That is the entire point, and it is what you will spend the next few hours learning to rely on.

Why not just Kafka and a database

Telemetry is not generic time-series data.

You can build a pipeline out of Kafka, InfluxDB and Grafana. Most teams do. What you then build by hand, and keep building, is everything that makes telemetry different.

A bare Kafka topicATLAS Open Streaming
a channel a field name an identity with units, sample rate, valid range and display format — carried in-band
a session a time window you agree on a first-class lifecycle, with its own configuration attached
multi-rate your problem modelled — periodic, row and synchro forms
laps & events a table you maintain separately timestamped markers on the stream
a late joiner your problem configuration is explicit, so you know what you missed

The usual symptom of not having this is a chart that has quietly been in the wrong units since somebody swapped a sensor eighteen months ago.

Curriculum

Nine modules. You write code in seven of them.

Each one has a task with a machine-checkable outcome. You work on a branch, open a pull request in your own repository, and a bot runs the whole pipeline against your work and tells you what it found.

Foundation

4 modules · about an evening
  • 01 Make it yoursBuilt

    Get a real pipeline running, and find out what a session actually is.

    graded — the session carries your handle, and the data reaches Kafka

  • 02 Add a channelBuilt

    Declare a parameter and emit it. Watch a consumer you did not modify learn its units.

    graded — a channel the lab does not ship appears, with units

  • 03 Fix the broken producerBuilt

    A variant produces jumpy traces. Nothing errors. Every value is correct. Find out why.

    graded — zero discontinuities across the session

  • 04 Write your first consumerBuilt

    Decode the stream yourself, in any language. The validator invents a new channel every run, so nothing can be hardcoded.

    graded against what the producer recorded sending

Practitioner

5 modules · 8–12 hours
  • 05Build a bridge

    Get an arbitrary source — a CSV, a public dataset, your own rig — into the stack with a clean session lifecycle.

  • 06Survive a late joiner

    Make a consumer that starts mid-session work anyway. Configuration was sent once, and you missed it.

  • 07Detect an event

    Find something real in the data — a lockup, an over-temperature — and mark it. Graded against the simulator's ground truth.

  • 08Instrument it

    Catch the failures that report success. The validator injects one and checks you noticed.

  • 09Conformance

    Malformed packets, a session ending mid-batch, interleaved sessions, a producer that dies without closing.
